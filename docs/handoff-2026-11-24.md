# YHFD Relay Pumping Calculator: handoff notes

Written 2026-11-24 by Claude (chat session) for Claude Code. Alex Vergo is the owner.

## What this is

One HTML page that plans a long supply line lay for Yorktown Heights Fire District (YHFD).
The problem: long driveways that climb hills. Hose length and elevation loss make relay pumping hard to plan.

The user traces a hose route on a map. The page reads the ground height along the route.
It then shows the hose to lay, the climb, the relay engines, each engine position, and each pump discharge pressure (PDP).
It shows 500, 750, and 1,000 GPM side by side. It also makes a one-page pre-plan card to print.

Status: pilot v0.2. Alex tested it and approved the direction. No field check is done yet.

## The three-part plan

## Hydraulic rules

All values come from the YHFD chauffeur reference chart (PDF).

- Friction loss, 5 inch hose, PSI for each 100 ft: 2 at 500 GPM, 5 at 750 GPM, 10 at 1,000 GPM.
- Friction loss, 4 inch hose: 5, 10, and 20.
- Formula option: `C x (GPM/100)^2 x (ft/100)`, with C = 0.08 for 5 inch and 0.2 for 4 inch.
- Climb: 0.5 PSI for each foot.
- Maximum LDH pressure: 200 PSI. The page warns when a downhill run goes above it.
- Minimum pressure in the hose at each point: 10 PSI.
- Intake pressure to hold at each engine: 20 PSI (setting: 20 or 50).
- Maximum PDP: 150 PSI (setting: 150, 175, or 200).
- Minimum PDP: 50 PSI, a pump at idle.
- PDP rounds to the nearest 5 PSI at or above the demand, with a 1 PSI tolerance.
- Hose length: map distance and slope, plus a slack allowance (setting, 10% default).
- Ground height: one sample each 25 ft, 400 samples maximum for a route.

Relay placement: the page finds the fewest engines at the maximum PDP.
It then lowers the pressure to the lowest equal value that keeps that engine count.
The user can drag a relay engine. The page then shows the PDPs for that position and flags a limit that is broken.

## Decisions Alex made

- Calculator pilot first, before the district screen.
- Show 500, 750, and 1,000 GPM together.
- The product is a planning tool plus printed cards.
- Hydrants come from public county data.
- A reverse lay / forward lay selector with no default. The user picks one for each new route.
- The lay sets the click order. Reverse: attack engine first, water source last. Forward: the opposite.
- The lay sets which engine lays each hose section. The page shows the lay order.
- Hard limit: no hose section is longer than the supply hose on one engine. The page adds relay engines to hold it.

The route points are always stored in water order: source first, attack engine last.

## Open items

1. **Hose loads.** The supply hose on each engine is a 1,000 ft placeholder. It now sets the engine count. Alex gets the real 5 inch load for each YHFD engine.
2. **Relay pressure policy.** 150 PSI or 200 PSI maximum PDP. Alex gets the department answer.
3. **Chart conflict 1.** The Max Relay Distance table in the chart PDF differs from the DOCX chart in the YHFD folder. The PDF values agree with the formula. The page uses the PDF. Alex confirms.
4. **Chart conflict 2.** The chart has two friction values for 5 inch hose at 1,000 GPM: 10 PSI in the table, 8 PSI from the formula. The page uses the table. A setting changes it.
5. **Field check.** Alex traces two or three driveways the chauffeurs know and compares the numbers.
6. **Find.** The district screen of long, steep driveways.
7. **Carry.** Cards for the street directory and SOG 4.02.

## Known limits

- The page does not know the flow a hydrant or draft site can give.
- It does not include appliance loss or attack line pressures.
- The hydrant data is from 2015.
- A forward lay needs one more engine at the water source. That engine lays no supply hose. The page says this in a note. It does not add it to an engine count.
- The page stores the last plan in the browser. A saved plan file (JSON) is the durable copy.
